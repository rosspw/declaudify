# Self-check

Run after every edit, silently. Pass or fail each line. Fix any fail, then run it again. Do not show this
list to the user.

## The point and the reader
1. Can a reader say, after the first paragraph (first sentence for a chat answer, first two lines for a
   post), what this is for and what it wants from them?
2. Does the opening of an answer or a doc stand alone in 50 words or fewer? (Scripts and personal posts
   are exempt.)
3. Is anything left in that the reader already has: a restated question, context they wrote, a proof they
   did not ask for?

## Structure and format
4. Did I read the format's section in `format-map.md` and apply its rules, not a generic set?
5. Does every heading earn its place (a section a reader jumps to), and does it say the content or the
   claim?
6. Are bullets only for parallel items, tables only for comparisons, bold only for the one thing a skimmer
   must see?
7. Did I keep structure the reader needs (a README's install section, a doc's table) rather than strip it
   because a list said bullets are a tell?
8. Is the touch budget I stated the one I actually spent?

## Facts
9. Is every item on the must-survive list present and unchanged: names, numbers, dates, quotes, claims,
   negations, caveats, links, paths, commands, code, and every attribution (author, owning team, speaker
   and how they are described)?
9a. Is every fact stated once? A fact moved into the opening is gone from where it was.
10. Did I add nothing: no fact, name, number, example, source, opinion or placeholder the text or the user
    did not give?
11. Did "only", "not", "unless", "about", "at least", rankings and sequences survive every shape edit?
11a. Did every negation that limits a claim ("not for unlimited energy", "it doesn't add") survive, and every
    marker of a guess ("we think", "we expect", "probably")? Cutting either changes what the writer claimed.
12. Is anything I could not keep or could not fix listed under Gaps, as a question?

## Dialect and words
13. Is every Claudish term translated, kept with a one-time plain explanation for a technical reader, or
    left alone because it is in a quote, a name, a command or a discussion of the word?
14. Does every reassurance word (cleanly, coherent, robust, verified, comprehensive) now name the check that
    was run, or is it gone?
15. Are the overused words gone where they added nothing, and kept where they are quoted, literal or the
    writer's voice?

## Tells
16. Act-on-sight tells: contrasts, closers, run-ups, deep sayings, arguing with no one, chatbot residue,
    status-report voice, writing about the document, weasel attribution, inflated significance, kickers,
    recap endings, engagement bait: all gone?
17. Cluster tells fixed where they clustered, and left alone where one stood by itself?
18. Weak-alone tells touched only when they had company?
19. Dashes within the rule for this format and this writer?
20. Did my edit create a new pattern: the same sentence length three times, every paragraph the same shape,
    a new row of short declaratives?

## Voice
21. Would the writer recognize this as theirs: vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, asides,
    level of polish?
22. Are strong human sentences left alone, even ones that break a rule?
23. Did the writer's sample or stated rules beat every default here?
24. For a script: does it sound like talking when read aloud?

## Output
25. Do the format line, the What changed bullets and the Gaps pass every rule above themselves?
26. Is What changed 3 to 6 bullets at the pattern level, not a diff of commas?
27. Audit mode: every row has a tell name with its tier, a quoted line and a short fix; no rewrite, no
    score, no guess about authorship?
28. Last answer mode: decision first, every fact, link, path and command kept, no status-report voice, no
    closing offer?
